Why this deal is structurally different

The city of Norfolk holds 73 acres at the Military Circle site with zero debt and acquired it for $10. A Community Land Trust vehicle permanently removes the land from speculation and from the capital stack — reducing project cost by an estimated 25–40% versus comparable market-rate development. This is not a subsidy play. It's a structural cost advantage that enables stabilized returns at below-market rents.

Market context — Norfolk housing

Norfolk-Virginia Beach MSA: HUD 4-person AMI $82,900 (2024). Market 2BR: ~$1,650/mo. CLT 60% AMI target: $1,244/mo. Norfolk has a structural housing shortage driven by defense-sector employment growth (Naval Station Norfolk is the world's largest naval base). Vacancy rates below 4%.

Military Circle is the only large, city-owned, shovel-adjacent site in the metro. No comparable land assembly opportunity exists.

Comparable transactions

Champlain Housing Trust (Burlington, VT): $60M+ portfolio, 2,500+ units, 30-year track record. CLT condos resell at affordable prices through 5+ ownership cycles. Gold standard for the model proposed here.

DC CLT / Banneker Commons: 230 units on city-owned land. Mixed LIHTC + city ground lease. Stabilized and cash-flow positive.

Atlanta Land Trust: Scattered-site CLT, $15M CDFI debt pool. Demonstrates CDFI appetite for CLT collateral in Southern markets.

Regulatory path — PD rezoning eliminates CUP uncertainty

Military Circle is zoned C-R (Regional Commercial). Under current zoning, any multi-family development over 24 units requires an individual Conditional Use Permit — a City Council vote. With ~200 parcels in the plan, the default path creates 200+ discrete political events over 20 years, each a potential veto point. This is the primary regulatory risk for investors.

The solution is a Planned Development (PD) district rezoning — a single City Council vote that establishes a master plan governing all subsequent development. The PD path is standard practice for large mixed-use sites in Norfolk: East Beach, Harbor Walk, and Broad Creek all used it. Once approved, individual parcel development proceeds under the master plan without further Council action. A PD rezoning converts 200 political events into 1. This is the enabling condition that must precede capital deployment.

The C-R district's stated purpose explicitly includes "high-density mixed-use development" — a PD application for Military Circle is consistent with the existing zoning intent and well-precedented in Norfolk's approval history.

MPB, Inc — the private operator and the 2036 horizon

The incumbent private mall operator, MPB, Inc, holds approximately 45 acres at the Military Circle site assessed at ~$230M, including a 15.6-acre spine parcel it purchased from the EDA in 2020 for $20M. MPB's holdings interleave with the 73-acre EDA portfolio. Ross Stores, a primary tenant, holds options on MPB space running to 2036.

Post-2036, MPB faces a stranded-asset scenario as mall retail declines nationally. The CLT structure offers MPB a ground-lease exit: guaranteed income on land that becomes an anchor drag. Early engagement — before any public redevelopment announcement inflates expectations — is the preferred approach. If successful, MPB's ~45 acres could expand the site to ~118 acres and increase the unit count by an estimated 30–40%. This is a Phase 4 scenario, not a Phase 1 dependency.

Path to investment

The plan requires city action first — rezoning and CLT charter establishment — before institutional capital can flow. The city's minimum viable commitment is a ground lease framework and a collaborative RFQ (not a master developer RFP). Once the CLT is chartered and the first parcel is leased, standard LIHTC + VHDA financing applies. The investment structure is well-precedented; the enabling policy is the bottleneck.

Investors interested in Phase 1 pipeline should engage now to shape the CLT charter terms, CDE selection for NMTC allocation, and parcel prioritization. Early-stage engagement secures allocation before competitive demand at Tide station announcement.
