# jason-edelman.org — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Developers, civic technologists, potential collaborators, and anyone who has stumbled
in from one of the subprojects (abolish-lawns, power-explained, mithlond, ghent-zoning-reform).
Context: desktop browsing, probably sent a link. Low intent — they're evaluating whether
to read further. Need: orientation, character, and a clear sense of what this person builds.

### Brand Personality
Three words: **grounded, rigorous, provisional**

Not a portfolio site. Not a startup founder's landing page. The record of someone who
builds things seriously and thinks carefully about why. The commons framework runs
through everything — this should feel like it was made by the same person who wrote
the manifesto and the policy briefs, not a different person who runs a website.

### Aesthetic Direction
The physical object: field notes in a Moleskine with printed margin tabs. A 1970s
government technical report annotated in pencil by an anarchist.

- **Dark by default.** Already correct. Do not lighten.
- **Green accent** (`#5fa86e` / `#7ec9a0`) — ecosystem, commons, organic. Already correct.
- **Typography:** Charis SIL body (Jason's pick, 2026-11-08), the open descendant of
  Matthew Carter's Charter (1987), drawn to survive crude printers: documentary rather than
  literary, sturdy under light-on-dark. It replaced Lora. Epilogue for display/headings. The
  library adds Courier Prime for typed fields (barcodes, dates, stamps).
- **Density:** Tight, dense. On desktop the page runs full width with the project cards
  in three columns (Jason, 2026-11-09); text keeps a reading measure, and phones stay one
  column. No hero sections. No cards with shadows. No rounded rectangles. No gradients.
- **Background** (Jason, 2026-11-11): conifers against cloud, as a faint light mask. A photo
  Jason chose (a mountain trail under conifers) is baked to its sky's brightness alone
  (`img/forest-mask.webp`: a pale green-grey whose alpha follows the photo's lightness), shown
  fixed behind the page at 10% (`body::before` in `style.css`). The cloud glows a little and
  the trees stay dark silhouettes. It replaced a WebGL sunfleck shader (2026-11-09 to 11-11)
  that read as dust on the screen. Plain CSS, no motion. It stays under the cards and never
  competes with text. Keep it that quiet.
- **"Write to me"** (Jason, 2026-11-09): the contact form waits behind one button whose face
  is dark pond water (`liquid.js`). The pointer drops ripples that spread and fade. Jason
  asked for "ripples on a pond and less lava lamp" after a metaball version, so keep it calm.
- **Trailers** (Jason, 2026-11-11: "a modal player that launches from the card image, with a large button on the
  card that launches the game"): a game card's picture carries a small "▶ Trailer · 0:17" tag and opens the trailer
  in a native `<dialog>` (`trailer.js`; without JavaScript the link opens the video file). Under the description, a
  large flat green "Play it in your browser" button launches the game. Videos and their poster frames live in
  `video/` (H.264, CRF 25, faststart; keep each well under Cloudflare's 25 MiB asset limit).
- **Atlas of Care** (Jason, 2026-11-14: "Do a design pass to match the root site style"): `/atlas-of-care/` uses the
  palette and type as they are. The page opens on two questions set as large buttons ("What do you do to hold your
  world together?", "Who holds your world together?"); the map follows at once, with no preamble (Jason: "lead with
  the map", then "make the what do you do front and center"). The map shows only what people have added: water sits
  below the page, an empty place is the card surface, a place where someone is takes the accent green in three flat
  steps, and the marks, the ring on someone vouched for and the lines between people are the library's amber. Nothing on it comes from outside the palette.
- **Anti-references:** Startup landing pages, portfolio sites with "hero" sections,
  generic tech blog aesthetics, anything with a navbar. This site has no navbar.
  The outbound "Elsewhere" links (LinkedIn, GitHub, Bluesky…) moved from the footer to the
  header (Jason, 2026-11-10): beside the name from 1200px, under the tagline below that.
  They're a masthead's contact line in small muted mono, not site navigation; keep them so.
- **Sections** (Jason, 2026-11-10): the projects sit in three groups, Civic & commons,
  Games & toys, and AI & agents, linked from the header under the tagline (green mono caps,
  like the section labels they jump to; in-page anchors, not a navbar). Siren and Dream of
  the Earth lead their groups as full-row cards, with the picture beside the words from
  1100px.
- **Front page vs workshop** (Jason, 2026-11-13: "I'd like to make a good first impression"):
  the front page shows only work a stranger can use or read today, live and public. Prototypes,
  private builds and projects set aside live on `/workshop/` in two groups, "Not yet released"
  and "Set aside", reached from a "Workshop" link in the header and a callout after the
  projects. Siren stays on the front page though it's invite-only: it's the current civic
  work, and the blurb links it. When something ships, move its card back.

### Design Principles
1. **Restraint is the argument.** The site should feel like the work, not like marketing.
2. **Every element earns its place.** If a visual element doesn't carry information,
   it probably shouldn't be there.
3. **Subprojects are first-class.** They aren't portfolio items — they're the thing.
   The personal bio is context for the projects, not the other way around.
4. **Typography does the work.** No decorative elements. Hierarchy comes from
   font size, weight, and spacing — not color blocks, icons, or borders.
5. **The dev journal is as important as the project list.** It's where the actual
   intellectual work is visible.
