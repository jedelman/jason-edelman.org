Atlantic Triangle

A connected-history timeline · England · the Gold Coast (Ghana) · America

A friend asked whether anything existed that visualized how a country's history — its geography, its resources, the outside powers that came for both — shapes its national psychology and its position in the world over time. Nothing quite like that turned up. This is a proof of concept, not the answer: one connected cluster of three places whose histories are inseparable from each other, laid out on a shared timeline.

England, the Gold Coast, and colonial/early America are not three parallel stories — they're one story told from three vantage points. English ships built forts on the Gold Coast to extract gold, then people; the people taken were forced into the plantation economies of English colonies in America; the wealth those colonies generated flowed back to England. The tracks below plot each place on its own axis; the flows crossing between them plot what actually moved from one to another — people, goods, money, and ideas.

Scope: this covers one cluster as a proof of concept — not "every country," which was the original, much larger ask. A second cluster (Mexico/Spain was suggested) is a natural next step if this format holds up. National borders shifted constantly over this span (the Ashanti Empire and Gold Coast are not the same polity as modern Ghana, no more than colonial Virginia is the modern United States); the tracks below follow the geographic region, not a fixed nation-state.
