Stories and interventions

Each sourced entry names a place, the people doing the work, what they do, who pays, and what happened when it stopped, as far as the source says. The quotes are copied from the linked pages. Links added by people come first and say who added them. The filters above the map apply here too.

About

There’s a whole geography of tending, an atlas of people quietly holding the world together, that I think is more visible to the people that want to profit from the chaos of losing than it is to the vaster population who is protected by it. What if it was made visible?
Jason Edelman, on Bluesky, 14 November 2026

Pick a layer, then pick a place. The layers are in order of how soon it is felt when the work behind them stops: a birth with nobody trained in the room is felt within hours, an untended forest over years. Three parts of the map open to local units: the counties of the United States, the districts of India and the regions of Europe.

The marks on top are particular: a place, the people doing the work, who pays, and a source. A ring is maintenance, which has to be done again or the loss comes back. A diamond is leverage, a bounded act that changes what follows. Where a famous result is disputed, the entry says so.

Anyone with an atproto account, such as a Bluesky handle, can add a link to a place: a web address and a location, nothing else. Links are the amber dots. Each carries the handle of whoever added it, and nobody has checked where it leads. Anyone signed in can ask for a link to be taken down, and it comes off this page at once.
