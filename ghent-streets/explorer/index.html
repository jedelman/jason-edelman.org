Illustrative. The storefront and everything on the sidewalk are drawn by this page, not a design for this building. Each one follows Norfolk's Historic District Design Guidelines (adopted by the Architectural Review Board, May 26, 2026): houses keep their open porches and their doors and windows where they are; storefronts go on commercial buildings; canvas awnings shade the first floor; signs sit on sign bands, windows and awnings, lit from outside. Building heights are approximate. Nothing here removes a tree: tables and signs make room for the city's trees.

Built from the City of Norfolk's open GIS (zoning, parcels, buildings, streets) and open data (addresses, FY26 assessments, the tree inventory). Each tree stands where the city mapped it, with the city's species and trunk diameter; its height and crown are drawn from the diameter, so they're approximate, and the inventory counts street and park trees, not most of the trees in back yards.
