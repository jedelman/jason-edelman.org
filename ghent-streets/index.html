The Problem

Historic protection shouldn't mean commercial prohibition.

Norfolk's HC-G2 district was designed to protect Ghent's historic residential fabric, and it does. But it also prohibits street-facing commercial activity outright. No café, no corner store, no bookshop, no neighborhood bar can open on Colonial Avenue: Table 3.6.11 leaves those uses blank in HC-G2, and a blank cell means prohibited. There's no permit to apply for, at any price. Only City Council can change that, by amending the table.

The result is what you see: a neighborhood of extraordinary houses behind dead sidewalks. Of the district's 420 parcels, nine are commercial, and under today's table no new shop can join them. The pedestrian life that should naturally attach to Ghent's dense, walkable grid isn't there. Residents want it, and the zoning code forbids it.

Nearby, Colley Avenue and 21st Street are alive. They have Pedestrian Commercial Overlay districts, and their ground floors produce tax revenue, foot traffic, and neighbors who know each other. HC-G2 has no such overlay. That's a policy failure, and it's fixable.

What We're Asking For

  • Permit neighborhood-scale retail uses by right on ground floors of parcels with street frontage in HC-G2
  • Use performance standards to manage scale, hours, and compatibility
  • Preserve ARB review for exterior alterations; don't require it for change of use alone
  • Model the amendment on Norfolk's existing PCO districts, which the city has already proven work
