About This Campaign

We have a fix.

Ghent Streets is a community-led effort to amend Norfolk's HC-G2 zoning district to permit neighborhood-scale retail by right. The tools to do this already exist inside the city's own code. We're just asking the city to use them.

Who We Are

Rooted in Ghent and working through the process.

This campaign was started by a member of the Ghent Neighborhood League. He knows the process and the players, and he knows the neighborhood well enough to see that the problem is the zoning code, not the architecture.

This is not a fight against historic preservation. Ghent's residential fabric is worth protecting, and that isn't in dispute. But protecting the Victorian streetscape doesn't require banning the corner coffee shop, the neighborhood bookstore, the wine bar, or the bodega.

The text amendment we're proposing would permit small-scale, ground-floor retail uses by right on parcels with street frontage. Norfolk has already used the same model successfully on Colley Avenue and 21st Street, through its Pedestrian Commercial Overlay district, so this isn't an experiment.

The Neighborhood

Extraordinary houses behind dead sidewalks.

Its National Register nomination calls Ghent "Norfolk's best-preserved, turn-of-the-century suburban development." It began in 1890, with most construction between 1892 and 1907, as one of the city's first planned residential suburbs, and its grid is dense and walkable by design. Urban planners spend decades trying to recreate bones like these.

And yet the sidewalks are dead. At 7pm on a weekday, Colonial Avenue feels like a suburban cul-de-sac with better cornices. The ground floors of dense urban neighborhoods naturally fill with a café, a dry cleaner, a wine shop, a florist. None of them can legally open here. Table 3.6.11 leaves those uses blank in HC-G2, and a blank cell means prohibited: there's no permit to apply for, at any price.

The result: nine commercial parcels among 420 (the 7-Eleven, a restaurant, two shops, two offices, one other commercial building and two parking lots), surrounded by dead frontage, broken stoops, and concentrated unhoused presence in the vacancies.

The Argument

Three things are true at once.

Historic preservation is legitimate. HC-G2's standards exist for good reason and should stay: ARB review, design guidelines, setback requirements. We are not asking to demolish anything, add a parking garage, or build a strip mall.

Active ground floors are compatible with historic character. Norfolk's own historic district design guidelines already cover storefronts, awnings and signs, because the city's historic districts include commercial buildings.

The current rule protects the wrong thing. The district's few commercial uses can stay, but under today's table none of them could open new, and neither can a neighbor's café or bookshop. That's backwards.

The Process

How a text amendment works in Norfolk.

A zoning text amendment in Norfolk goes through three stages: a recommendation from the Architectural Review Board, a public hearing before the Planning Commission, and a final vote by City Council. Only City Council or the Planning Commission can initiate one (Zoning Ordinance §2.3.2.B(3)(a)(iii)), so residents ask the Commission to.

The Planning Department contact for a text amendment is Jeremy Sharp: (757) 439-4833 or jeremy.sharp@norfolk.gov. The relevant ordinance sections are Table 3.6.11 (HC district uses), Table 3.9.23 (PCO uses), § 2.3.2 (text amendments), and the Norfolk Zoning Procedures Manual Section 3.3(B).

The relevant planning document is NFK2050, the comprehensive plan City Council adopted in December 2025. Its Future Land Use Map puts Ghent in the "Sustain – Traditional" place type, where the plan lists small-scale commercial and retail as a supported secondary use, encourages "small-scale retail or other complementary community-facing uses … at key nodes or intersections," and commits the city to "support new, naturally occurring local businesses that serve the community." The plan prefers main streets and key intersections to interior blocks, and the amendment as drafted goes further than that. We're asking Norfolk to act on values it already has.

What This Campaign Is Asking For

  • Amend Table 3.6.11 to permit neighborhood-scale retail uses by right on ground floors of parcels with street frontage in HC-G2
  • Use performance standards (hours, square footage, signage) to manage compatibility
  • Clarify that a change of use alone doesn't need a Certificate of Appropriateness; Virginia Code § 15.2-2306 ties ARB approval to erecting, altering or restoring buildings and signs
  • Model the amendment on Norfolk's existing PCO language, which is already proven and defensible
  • Establish this as precedent for other HC sub-districts facing the same structural problem
