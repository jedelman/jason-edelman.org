Case Study · Colley Avenue

1012 Colley Avenue

The end house of a 1908 row, on a street the City classes as an arterial or collector, one lot from the corner of Graydon Avenue. Its front room could hold a bookshop. NFK2050 points a neighborhood's shops toward streets like this one, and HC-G2's zoning prohibits them.

The City's assessor has photographed 1012 Colley three times, in 2007, August 2025 and June 2026. It is painted white brick, two floors over a raised base. A stoop with dark railings runs along the front, a black door with a transom stands beside a wide window, the upstairs windows have dark shutters, and there are dormers in the roof. The City's building footprint draws it as one building with its row: 1014 Colley on the corner, and four houses on Graydon Avenue.

What the front room could hold

We built the house by hand for the parcel explorer, from the City's building footprint and these photos. The City draws the whole row as one footprint, so the explorer cuts out this house's share and leaves the rest of the row standing. Pick a use and the front room fills: lettering on the wide window under a canvas awning, a sign hung in the porch, lanterns at the door, a book cart or a bench on the stoop. The house itself doesn't change. The doors and windows stay where they are, as the Architectural Review Board's design guidelines ask of a house.

The rule

1012 Colley is in HC-G2. Table 3.6.11 of the zoning ordinance leaves restaurants, retail goods establishments and offices blank in HC-G2, and a blank means prohibited. No permit can authorize them, at any price. Across the district line in HC-G3, the same table allows all three: offices by right, and restaurants and retail by right or with a conditional use permit.

The plan

NFK2050, the comprehensive plan City Council adopted on December 9, 2025, puts HC-G2 in its Sustain – Traditional place type. For that place type it lists "Commercial/retail, small-scale office" as a secondary land use (p. 92), and it says where secondary uses belong: "The Secondary, non-residential uses envisioned for the Sustain areas are appropriate primarily along arterials and collector streets, preserving the residential character of the neighborhood interiors" (p. 69).

The City's own street layer classes Colley Avenue in front of 1012 as an arterial or collector street. This is the frontage that passage describes. The plan also encourages small-scale retail "at key nodes or intersections, with consistent street frontage" (p. 93). 1012 isn't the corner house, but it's the next one: its neighbor in the row, 1014 Colley, stands at the corner of Colley and Graydon.

What it would take

Policy Brief No. 1 proposes amending Table 3.6.11 to permit six small uses by right in HC-G2, under performance standards. As drafted, the amendment reaches the interior blocks too, which the plan says to keep residential. On a block like this one, the plan and the amendment agree.

This is an illustration, not a proposal for this property. We haven't spoken with the people who live here, and nothing on this page says they want a bookshop in their front room. It shows what the rule rules out, on a house the City has photographed for nineteen years.
