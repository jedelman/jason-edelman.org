Policy Brief · No. 1

The Case for By-Right Retail in HC-G2

A proposed zoning text amendment to permit neighborhood-scale retail uses on ground floors in Norfolk's Ghent Historic and Cultural Conservation district, with the planning, legal, and economic argument for it.

Norfolk, Virginia HC-G2 District Zoning Ordinance § 3.6 / Table 3.6.11 NFK2050 — Sustain – Traditional Version 1.0 · 2025

Executive Summary

Norfolk's HC-G2 zoning district covers the historic core of Ghent, which its National Register nomination calls "Norfolk's best-preserved, turn-of-the-century suburban development." Its zoning prohibits street-facing retail, restaurant, and personal service uses on ground floors outright: Table 3.6.11 leaves them blank, and no permit can authorize them. This brief argues that the prohibition is a policy failure with real costs, and proposes amendment language for Table 3.6.11 that would permit six uses by right, subject to performance standards: neighborhood retail, small restaurants and cafés, personal services, ground-floor professional offices (medical and dental included), art galleries and studios, and small fitness studios. That goes further than Norfolk's five Pedestrian Commercial Overlay districts, where restaurants and personal services are permitted when performance standards are met, and retail goods on only two of the five. NFK2050, the comprehensive plan City Council adopted in December 2025, supports small-scale retail in Ghent at "key nodes or intersections" and "primarily along arterials and collector streets, preserving the residential character of the neighborhood interiors." As drafted, the amendment also reaches interior blocks, which the plan says to keep residential (Section VII).

I. What HC-G2 Currently Allows

Norfolk's Historic and Cultural Conservation districts are governed by Article 3, Section 3.6 of the Zoning Ordinance, with permitted uses listed in Table 3.6.11. Under the current table, HC-G2 permits the following as principal uses by right (P): single-family and limited multi-family residential uses, institutional uses such as schools and churches, and home occupations subject to performance standards.

The following uses are currently prohibited in HC-G2: their cells are blank. A blank cell in the use table is not a default or an ambiguity. The table key states it explicitly: "BLANK CELL = PROHIBITED." (Norfolk Zoning Ordinance, Table 3.6.11 key.)

  • Eating and drinking establishments of any kind
  • Retail sales, general
  • Personal services (barbers, salons, tailors, etc.)
  • Office uses in ground-floor street-facing positions
  • Neighborhood-serving commercial uses of any kind

The practical consequence: any entrepreneur who wants to open a coffee shop, corner store, bookshop, or dry cleaner on Colonial Avenue or any other HC-G2 street has no application to file. No conditional use permit or fee authorizes a prohibited use; the only route is an amendment to the ordinance adopted by City Council. Nobody opens a café by waiting on a text amendment, and the streets stay dead.

"In HC-G2 there is no permit for a café, at any price. The only remedy is to amend the table."

II. The Cost of Dead Streets

These are the costs we see, drawn from the planning tradition that runs back to Jane Jacobs and from what neighbors see on the ground in Ghent. They are arguments, not measured findings:

Loss of informal surveillance. Jane Jacobs identified "eyes upon the street" as the primary mechanism of urban safety. Commercial frontage generates foot traffic through the day; residential-only blocks generate less. We think unhoused people concentrate on HC-G2 streets partly because the district lacks the active commercial uses that would generate natural foot traffic and informal community presence.

Lost tax revenue. Ground-floor businesses pay meals, sales and business license taxes that houses don't. Whether they also raise nearby home values is less clear; the research is mixed (Policy Brief No. 2, Section IV-A). Policy Brief No. 2's scenario model puts the gross figure at about $227,000 a year from 15 new ground-floor businesses and about $602,000 from 40, mostly meals tax, before subtracting spending that would have happened elsewhere in Norfolk anyway.

Loss of neighborhood-serving amenities. Residents of HC-G2 leave the district for goods and services that a walkable neighborhood would have on its own corners. That means more driving and fewer chance meetings between neighbors.

Quieter parks. In our view, Ghent's parks see less use than they could because the streets around them generate little foot traffic. Retail on adjacent blocks would bring some.

III. The PCO Precedent

Norfolk has already created mixed residential-commercial streets through its Pedestrian Commercial Overlay (PCO) districts. Five corridors carry PCO designations: Colley Avenue, 21st Street, Riverview, 35th Street and Lafayette Boulevard. In all five, restaurants and personal services are P/C under Table 3.9.23, permitted when they meet the overlay's performance standards, with a conditional use permit only where those standards call for one; retail goods establishments get the same treatment on Colley Avenue and 21st Street. Along all of Colley Avenue, which the overlay covers in part, 106 of 229 parcels are commercial.

HC-G2 has the same density, walkability, and demand as these corridors. It lacks only the zoning permission. The amendment proposed here would bring HC-G2 into alignment with what the city has already proven works, without requiring a new overlay district, a rezoning, or any change to the protections for residential character that HC-G2 also provides.

IV. The ARB Question

Any proposal to amend zoning requirements in historic districts requires a recommendation from the Architectural Review Board before it proceeds to the City Planning Commission. This is appropriate, because the ARB exists to protect the physical character of the district, and that function should be preserved.

This proposal is designed to work with ARB authority, not around it:

  • The amendment permits uses by right. It does not permit physical alterations by right.
  • Any exterior modification associated with a new commercial use, such as signage, facade changes, or new windows, still requires a Certificate of Appropriateness from the ARB.
  • The ARB's design review authority is unchanged. The amendment changes the use table: a café in an existing ground-floor space would be permitted by right, though it would still require a zoning certificate and, for any exterior work, ARB review.

The amendment expands use rights without expanding rights to physical modification.

V. Proposed Text Amendment Language

The following is proposed amendment language for Table 3.6.11: Principal, Accessory, and Temporary Use Table for Historic and Cultural Conservation Base Zoning Districts.

For each use category listed below, we propose changing the HC-G2 column from blank (prohibited) to P (permitted by right), subject to the performance standards in Section VI below.

TABLE 3.6.11 PROPOSED AMENDMENTS — HC-G2 COLUMN USE CATEGORY CURRENT PROPOSED ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── COMMERCIAL USES — RETAIL & SERVICE Retail sales, neighborhood-serving [blank] P (grocery, pharmacy, hardware, books, gifts, clothing — no single use to exceed 3,500 sq ft ground floor GFA) Eating and drinking establishment, [blank] P small-scale (no drive-through; no more than 50 seats; no live entertainment without CUP) Personal services [blank] P (barber, salon, tailor, dry cleaner, copy center, similar) Professional services, ground-floor [blank] P (financial, legal, medical, dental — ground floor only; street-facing window display required) Art gallery / studio [blank] P Fitness studio, small-scale [blank] P (yoga, martial arts, dance — no more than 3,000 sq ft) NOTES: • All uses above are PRINCIPAL uses permitted BY RIGHT (P) on parcels with ground-floor street frontage in HC-G2. • All uses are subject to performance standards in Section 4.3.3.X (new section — see Section VI below). • Exterior alterations to structures remain subject to Certificate of Appropriateness (ARB review). • Drive-through facilities remain prohibited in HC-G2. • Large-format retail (>3,500 sq ft GFA) would be C (CUP). • Bars/taverns (primary alcohol sales) would be C (CUP). • Adult uses remain prohibited.

VI. Proposed Performance Standards (New Section 4.3.3.X)

The following performance standards shall apply to all neighborhood-commercial uses permitted by right under the amended Table 3.6.11 in HC-G2:

  1. Ground floor only. Commercial uses are permitted on ground floors only. Upper floors of any structure shall remain residential or institutional.
  2. Street-facing activation. Existing street-facing doors and windows shall be retained in their current locations; this section requires no new openings and no minimum share of glazing. Interior activity shall be visible from the sidewalk through the existing windows.
  3. Size limits. No single retail or eating/drinking establishment shall exceed 3,500 square feet of ground-floor gross floor area without approval of a Conditional Use Permit.
  4. Hours. Standard hours of operation are 7:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m. Extended hours (to 2:00 a.m.) require a CUP for uses within 100 feet of a residential use.
  5. Signage. Signage shall comply with Section 5.7 of the Zoning Ordinance and, for any exterior signage, shall require a Certificate of Appropriateness from the ARB.
  6. Outdoor seating. Outdoor seating on private property is permitted. Outdoor seating extending into the public right-of-way requires a separate encroachment permit.
  7. Parking. No minimum off-street parking requirement for uses under 2,000 sq ft GFA. Uses over 2,000 sq ft: one space per 500 sq ft. Bicycle parking: two spaces minimum per establishment.
  8. Deliveries. Commercial deliveries shall not block travel lanes and shall be completed between 7:00 a.m. and 8:00 p.m.

VII. Consistency with NFK2050

City Council adopted NFK2050 as Norfolk's comprehensive plan on December 9, 2025 (Ordinance No. 50201), repealing plaNorfolk2030. The following are verbatim from the adopted plan:

  • Place type. The NFK2050 Future Land Use Map designates Ghent's historic district Sustain – Traditional. The plan describes these areas as "predominantly single family, with a mix of missing middle housing and small commercial amenities that have integrated over time" (p. 92).
  • Uses. For Sustain – Traditional, the plan lists "Commercial/retail, small-scale office" as a secondary land use (p. 92). Secondary uses are "uses that either are allowed through an entitlement process or require alterations to the land use regulations or zoning map to be permitted" (p. 69). This text amendment is that alteration.
  • Where. "Small-scale retail or other complementary community-facing uses are encouraged at key nodes or intersections, with consistent street frontage" (p. 93). For Sustain areas generally: "The Secondary, non-residential uses envisioned for the Sustain areas are appropriate primarily along arterials and collector streets, preserving the residential character of the neighborhood interiors. Non-residential uses in the Sustain Place Types are characterized by small-scale, neighborhood-serving development at a scale and form that is consistent with the applicable Character District" (p. 69).
  • Goal. By 2050, Sustain – Traditional places will "Preserve local businesses and support new, naturally occurring local businesses that serve the community" (p. 92).

The plan supports small-scale, neighborhood-serving retail in Ghent and says where it fits: at key nodes or intersections, and primarily along arterial and collector streets, preserving the residential character of neighborhood interiors. Several HC-G2 streets carry the arterial or collector classification in the City's street centerline data, among them W Olney Road, W Princess Anne Road, Colley Avenue, Mowbray Arch, and parts of Colonial and Redgate Avenues. The amendment as drafted also reaches interior blocks, which the plan says to keep residential. Limiting it to arterial and collector frontage and key intersections would match the plan; keeping the wider scope means relying on the ground-floor limit, size caps and hours standards in Section VI to hold those uses to small scale.

VIII. Process and Timeline

A zoning text amendment to Table 3.6.11 proceeds as follows under Norfolk's Zoning Ordinance:

  1. Initiation. A text amendment "may only be initiated by resolution of the City Council or motion of the Planning Commission" (Zoning Ordinance §2.3.2.B(3)(a)(iii)). Residents and organizations ask the Commission to initiate one; in 2014 the Park Place Business Association did so for the 35th Street PCO. Because this amendment touches an HC district, a pre-application neighborhood meeting is required (§2.3.2.B(2)). Fee: $915 for a zoning text amendment (Norfolk Planning Fee Schedule, effective July 1, 2024).
  2. ARB referral. Because the amendment modifies requirements in a historic district, the ARB must provide a recommendation before the Planning Commission public hearing. The ARB meets monthly; allow 30–45 days for scheduling.
  3. Planning Commission public hearing. Staff prepares a report; public notice is published in The Virginian-Pilot two weeks prior. The commission votes to recommend approval or denial to City Council.
  4. City Council vote. Council holds a final public hearing and votes. Approval requires a simple majority.

Estimated total timeline: 4–8 months from application to Council vote, assuming no significant opposition delays scheduling.

IX. Anticipated Objections and Responses

"This will change the residential character of the neighborhood." The amendment permits commercial uses on ground floors only, with size caps, signage controls, and all ARB exterior review retained. The physical character of the district, which the HC designation exists to protect, is unchanged. The use of a building can change without any exterior modification.

"There isn't demand." The displacement is visible on the ground: Colley Avenue, Granby Street, and the HC-G1 corridor absorb the commercial demand that HC-G2 cannot legally serve. Residents of HC-G2 patronize those businesses, so the demand exists and is expressed elsewhere. The commercial uses already operating in and beside the district, the 7-Eleven on Colonial and the Harris Teeter just outside it, are more direct evidence: they show that HC-G2 residents generate commercial activity at sufficient density to sustain retail. The constraint is supply, not demand.

"These uses should get case-by-case review." Conditional use permits are appropriate for uses whose impacts cannot be adequately managed by performance standards, and the amendment keeps them where they fit: larger establishments, bars and late hours would require a CUP (Sections V and VI). Small, ground-floor neighborhood retail does not need one: the impacts are well understood, the precedent is extensive (every PCO corridor in Norfolk), and the performance standards proposed here set size, hours, signage, outdoor seating, parking and delivery rules directly.

"This is better handled as a PCO overlay." A PCO overlay on specific corridors is a valid alternative approach. The difference is scope: a PCO would apply only to designated streets; this amendment applies HC-G2-wide, treating the entire district as the walkable urban neighborhood it is rather than carving out selected corridors for different treatment. The PCO approach could be pursued in parallel as a fallback.

"If there were demand, someone would have asked by now." There is nothing to ask for. These uses are prohibited in HC-G2, so an operator has no application to file, and the absence of applications measures the rule, not the demand. The demand that exists goes to Colley Avenue, Granby Street and 21st Street, where the zoning lets it land. The absence of named operators in this brief reflects the current state of the campaign, not the absence of demand; gathering testimony from operators who considered HC-G2 and went elsewhere is the campaign's next step.

X. Recommendation

We ask the City Planning Commission to initiate, by motion, a text amendment to Table 3.6.11 of the Zoning Ordinance consistent with the language proposed in Section V of this brief. We further request that the Architectural Review Board for the Ghent Historic District be presented with this proposal at its next available meeting and be invited to provide a recommendation to the Planning Commission.

The amendment is modest, it has precedent in Norfolk's overlays, and NFK2050 supports it on main streets and at key intersections. It will not transform Ghent overnight, but it will lift the prohibition that currently prevents the neighborhood from being what its residents, its density, and its architecture naturally want it to be: a place where people walk, gather, and stay.
