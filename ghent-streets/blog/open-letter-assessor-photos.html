An Open Letter on the Assessor's Photographs

The City photographs its buildings street by street, some of them three times since 2007. The assessor's office made those photos, at public expense, as part of the public record. The City's own Open Data Policy puts published City data in the public domain, and we're asking the City to publish the photos that way.

Norfolk Open Data Policy §§ 2, 3.1, 5.2 November 2026

To the Office of the Real Estate Assessor and the Chief Information Officer, City of Norfolk

Your office photographs Norfolk's buildings from the street. In Ghent, many buildings have two or three of your photos on file: the oldest from 2007, the newest from last month. Taken together, they are the best record anyone has of what this city looks like and how it has changed.

We're building a 3D explorer of Ghent's historic district from the City's open data: its zoning, parcels, buildings and streets, and its tree inventory, down to the species and trunk size of the street trees. For the corner lots it shows your photos, credited to your office, so a neighbor can see the building as it stands before imagining what its ground floor could hold.

Your photos are the one part we can't use freely. As far as we can find, the City has never said they're restricted. They reach the public through the AIR property viewer, and the only terms posted there are its vendor's. Those terms reserve all rights in the content and forbid the use of scripts. The City's Open Data Policy says the opposite about City data:

“Proactively release publishable City data, making it freely available in appropriately varied and useful open formats, using an open license with no restrictions on use or reuse …”Open Data Policy § 3.1(a)
“Published datasets shall be placed into the public domain.”Open Data Policy § 5.2

The policy defines data as information “maintained or created by or on behalf of a City agency,” and it names federal copyright among the legal encumbrances open data should be free of. We read your photographs as exactly that kind of data. So we're asking for three things:

  1. Publish the assessor's street photographs, with the building sketches that go with them, as a dataset on Norfolk Open Data, in the public domain, as § 5.2 provides.
  2. Keep the older photos beside the new ones. The policy already commits the City to “free, historical archives of all released City data” (§ 3.1(c)). With a 2007 photo next to a 2026 photo, a neighborhood can see its own history.
  3. Until then, say plainly that residents may republish the photos with credit to your office.

Some of the photos show parked cars and people passing by. We'll blur license plates in any photo we host, and we'd understand if the City wanted to do the same before publishing.

In the meantime, the explorer loads your photos from the City's own server and credits your office. For a few buildings we'd like to go further and keep copies on our site, so the 3D models can wear the photos, trees and hedges included. We'll do that in the public interest, with credit, and with plates blurred. If you'd like any photo taken down, write to me at jason-edelman.org or on Bluesky, and it will come down.

Jason Edelman
Ghent Streets

SOURCES: City of Norfolk, Open Data Policy, §§ 2.1, 2.3, 3.1(a), 3.1(c), 5.2 (norfolk.gov/DocumentCenter/View/31912); Schneider Geospatial, Software Terms (the terms linked from air.norfolk.gov's disclaimer; Schneider acquired Spatialest, the assessor viewer's software, in August 2023); photo dates read from the assessor's photos of Ghent buildings, 2007–2026, on gisshare.norfolk.gov. All checked November 14, 2026.
