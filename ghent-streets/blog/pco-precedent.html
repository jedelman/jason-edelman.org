The PCO Model: What Norfolk Already Knows How to Do

Colley Avenue and 21st Street have Pedestrian Commercial Overlays, and their ground floors are full of businesses. The model is proven. Here's why it should extend to HC-G2.

PCO Districts · Norfolk Zoning Ordinance § 3.9 Table 3.9.23 2025

When advocates argue for zoning reform, skeptics often ask: "Has this been done before? Do we have evidence it works?" In Norfolk's case, we don't have to look at Portland or Minneapolis or some other city's planning experiment. We just have to walk five minutes, to Colley Avenue.

Norfolk's Pedestrian Commercial Overlay (PCO) districts are the direct precedent for what we're asking in HC-G2. They're in the zoning ordinance, and they work by every measure the city uses to evaluate commercial corridors. They were created by the same mechanism we're proposing to use here.

Pedestrian Commercial Overlays

A Pedestrian Commercial Overlay is a designation layered on top of a base zoning district. The base district (residential, historic, whatever) continues to govern the overall character and use of the area. The PCO adds a supplemental permission: ground-floor commercial uses are permitted by right along designated street frontages.

Norfolk currently has five PCO designations, each named for the corridor they govern: PCO-Colley, PCO-21st Street, PCO-Riverview, PCO-35th Street, and PCO-Lafayette. Each has its own section of the ordinance's text and is drawn on the Official Zoning Map, so creating or extending one takes a text amendment and a rezoning. This campaign is asking for less: a text amendment alone, to Table 3.6.11.

Table 3.9.23 of the Zoning Ordinance is the PCO use table. In all five PCO columns, offices, artist studios and galleries are P, permitted by right. Restaurants and personal services are P/C: permitted when they meet the overlay's performance standards, with a conditional use permit only where those standards call for one. Retail goods establishments get the same P/C on Colley Avenue and 21st Street, and are prohibited in the other three.

Colley Avenue on a Wednesday evening

Walk down Colley between 21st and 26th Street on a Wednesday evening. You will pass: independent restaurants, a wine bar, coffee shops, a bookstore, salons, a yoga studio, specialty retail, and gallery spaces. The sidewalks have foot traffic, the lights are on, and people are sitting outside. Neighbors who have never met are sharing the same block at the same time.

What separates Colley from Colonial Avenue isn't better architecture, and it isn't that Colley residents are more social or more entrepreneurial. The physical neighborhoods are comparable. The zoning code permits those uses to exist on Colley and prohibits them on Colonial.

"Colley Avenue is not a success story about a particular place. It's a success story about a particular policy. The policy is replicable."

A corridor overlay or a table amendment

We've considered two approaches: adding a PCO overlay to specific corridors within HC-G2, or amending Table 3.6.11 directly to permit commercial uses district-wide. Both achieve the goal on the corridors. They differ in scope and ambition.

A corridor PCO overlay, say a PCO-Colonial or PCO-Ghent designation on Colonial Avenue, would activate that specific street and leave the rest of HC-G2 unchanged. This is the narrower, politically safer path, and it is a genuine option if the broader amendment faces resistance.

Amending Table 3.6.11 directly treats HC-G2 as a dense urban residential district where the entire street grid is walkable, not just selected corridors. Any ground-floor, street-facing space in the district could then open for business, subject to performance standards, without the city pre-selecting winners and losers among streets. This is more ambitious, and it makes the more complete argument: the prohibition is wrong for the whole district, not just the most visible blocks.

Our position: pursue the Table 3.6.11 amendment as the primary ask. Identify PCO corridor designations on Colonial and other specific streets as a fallback that we will accept and build on. Don't give up the table amendment without a fight, but don't lose the campaign over tactics.

The process Norfolk has already walked through

Creating and changing PCOs has gone through the same steps this campaign is asking for: a City Planning Commission hearing and a City Council vote, with an ARB recommendation where a historic district is involved. When the 35th Street PCO's text was amended in 2014, at the Park Place Business Association's request, the staff report pointed to plaNorfolk2030's call for revisions to the PCO districts.

We need the same process and a plan consistency finding under NFK2050. We're not asking Norfolk to invent something new or take a regulatory risk it hasn't already taken five times. We are asking it to extend a model it created, deployed, and has observed working for years.

The hearing room argument is simple: You've done this before. Do it here.

For the ARB presentation

On a text amendment that touches a historic district, the ARB's role is to recommend (Zoning Ordinance §2.3.2.B(5)(a)), and its concerns are physical: signage, awnings, facade changes and outdoor seating.

That's the right framing for our ARB presentation too. We're not asking the ARB to approve commercial uses; use permissions are a Planning Commission and Council matter. We're asking the ARB to weigh in on whether the performance standards we've proposed (described in Policy Brief No. 1) adequately protect the physical character of the HC-G2 district. We believe they do. We want the ARB to agree, and we should come to that meeting prepared to revise the standards in response to their concerns rather than defending every word.

Bring precedents from the other PCO presentations. Bring photographs of what activated ground floors look like in HC-G1 corridors that already operate commercially. Make the visual case that commercial use and architectural integrity are not in conflict.

SOURCES: Norfolk Zoning Ordinance Table 3.9.23 (PCO Use Table); Norfolk Zoning Ordinance § 3.9 (Overlay Districts), § 3.9.10.B (the five PCO districts, shown on the Official Zoning Map), § 2.3.2 (text amendments); 35th Street PCO text amendment staff report (2014); Riverview PCO rezoning staff report (2016); Day Care Home Text Amendment staff report (Dec. 2023), demonstrating standard process for HC district text amendments; vacation rental text amendment staff reports (2020, 2023), demonstrating ARB role in HC district amendments.
